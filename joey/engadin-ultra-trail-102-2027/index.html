Engadin Ultra Trail — EUT102. Around the upper Engadin from Samedan and back: up to the Fuorcla Val Champagna at 2818 m in the first night, then over the valley sides to La Punt, Zuoz, the Es-cha hut and Bever, never lower than 1665 m. 102 km, 5677 m of climbing and the same again of descent, in seven big climbs and seven big drops. Start Friday 16 July 2027 at 23:00; 25 hours to finish.

Your goal, in your order: arrive at the start healthy, enjoy the race, and finish it. You have stood on start lines before already injured or overtrained, knowing you would stop. This plan is built so that does not happen again — which is why it starts at the load you already carry and rises slowly, and why its rest days are not optional.

km-effort puts different courses on one scale: every 100 m of climb counts as one more flat kilometre. The EUT102 is 158.7 km-effort. Your Falcotrail rate held for the whole race would be about 18 h 20′ — so a 22-23 h finish asks for less speed than you already have, held for longer. That is durability, not speed, and it is what this plan builds: being able to still run the runnable ground at hour fifteen.

One exception, and it is the race's sharpest risk: the first barrier. Punt Muragl, at about km 26, closes at 04:00 — five hours after the start, all of it in the dark, with 1400 m of climbing and 1400 m of descent before it. It asks more than the finish does: about 8 km-effort an hour. At your fresh, daylight rate you would reach it with 10-30 minutes to spare, and at night, on descents, less. So the plan does two things about it from the spring: night running on runnable trail and on descents becomes a trained skill, and you rehearse the first 26 km of the course at night before July.
What decides your race is not fitness. It is four things: staying uninjured; descending — seven drops of 800-1100 m, the biggest from 2818 m at km 35, and the thing you find hardest; the dark; and eating enough to train. The plan is built around all four.

B0 · Foundation · 8 weeks

From 10-15 hours a week of all your sports, about 1000 m of climbing and a longest run of 33 km in the last three months. The table below is the plan itself, redrawn at every rebuild; your current week is highlighted.

One thing rises at a time. Each week either the hours, the climbing or the long run goes up — never two together — and every fourth week is a down week, about a third lighter, with the same number of days. It is deliberately slower than you would choose. You have told us you are bad at resting and that injuries before the start are your history; the plan takes that seriously so you do not have to fight it every week.
Friday is a rest day, and a rest day is not a short run. No run, no gym, no "just a short one" — your own request, and the plan holds you to it. A walk is fine. The adaptation from the week happens on that day, not in spite of it.
Read the "longest D−" column as pulses. It is the longest single descent of the week — short and controlled now, while the right ankle reloads, and rising in pulses: every third build week a bigger one, the weeks between smaller, none in down weeks. Take it on the long run, at the end, at an easy effort. Descending is the skill this race asks most of you, and it is trained like any other: little, often, and growing.
The Anillo Minero, 5 December — a rehearsal, with a cost. It starts at 05:00, so three hours by headlamp: the dark, your kit, eating every hour from the first, your feet. It is gentle ground, but 70 km is about twice this block's long run, so it costs two to three weeks of recovery — which is why December is the recovery block. The 13-hour limit is not a walk: at your Falcotrail rate it is about 11 h 15′. By Sunday 22 November, decide: all 70 km, or stop at the km 37 relay point and call it a long run. If the ankle, the legs or your appetite are not right, km 37 is the race.

What the efforts mean

This plan prescribes duration and effort — never anything else. Effort is read by the talk test, and reading it well is a skill.

RPEThe talk test — say it out loudWhat it is for
2–3Full conversation. You could keep going all day.Recovery, warm-ups, walking
3–4Full sentences, aware of your breathing.Most of the plan — easy runs and long runs both
5Sentences, but you would rather not.The end of a long run, climbs
6–7Short phrases only.Sustained climbs — from the winter block, once a week
8–9A word or two.Rare, short, later — never in the last three weeks
So "easy 45′ RPE 3-4" means forty-five minutes at an effort where you could talk the whole way. If you cannot say a full sentence out loud, slow down — on a climb, walk. That is not a soft target: most of every week belongs at 3-4, and a week that is all medium is too hard to recover from and too easy to adapt to.

Running effort

  • Asks how hard is this to keep going? — the talk test, above.
  • The next morning's legs are the best check: an "RPE 4" that leaves you at 5/10 the next day was not an RPE 4.
  • Heat, cold, altitude, poor sleep and a hard week make the same effort come slower. That is the scale working.

Gym effort — a different scale

  • Asks how many more good reps you could still have done when you put the weight down.
  • RPE = 10 − reps in reserve. RPE 6 = four more, comfortably; RPE 8 = two more. Never 10: no set to failure.
  • It chooses the weight for a fixed number of reps — it is not an effort rating.
The same number means opposite things on the two scales. Running RPE 6 is hard — short phrases only. Gym RPE 6 is comfortable — four reps still in the tank. Carry the running meaning into the gym and you lift far too heavy.
You prefer jumping and reps to heavy weights — the box jumps, the single-leg deadlifts, the step-ups. Those stay; the plan adds slow, controlled descending work (step-downs, slow lowering), because strong legs on the way down are what this race asks for. Two sessions a week, each about 45 minutes, each one on its card with every exercise, its weight, its cue and a video.

What to eat, by the kind of day

The plan gives when and what — portions you already eat, no numbers. The rule underneath all of it is simple: a training day is a day to eat more, and a long day is a day to eat all day.

Settled, and still open

Decided

  • Effort by the talk test, never anything else. Your choice, and the system's: on ground that swings from runnable to hands-on-knees, effort is the only honest measure.
  • A rest day a week, and it is a rest day. Your request — the plan holds you to it.
  • Cross-country skiing and cycling are part of the plan, with their own lines in the weekly budget — skiing from the first snow, bikepacking when it fits. Swimming is a recovery option whenever you want it.
  • Mornings. Weekday sessions are planned for 05:30, strength included; the long run is at the weekend.
  • The plan proposes the days; the long run and the strength days are fixed. You asked for a fixed weekly plan: the shape of each week is the same — strength Tuesday and Thursday, rest Friday, long Saturday — and the rest adapts to what you actually did, so a missed or extra session is absorbed instead of piling up.

Watched

  • The right ankle. Watched from day one: descents short and controlled at first, and anything sharp, one-sided or changing how you run is said in the form the same day.
  • Your foot history. Impact rises slowly — two weeks at each new step before the next.
  • Anything in the chest — pressure, pain, palpitations, unusual breathlessness or dizziness during or after exercise — stops the session, and the doctor comes before the next one. Not a training question.
  • The first barrier at 04:00. The race's sharpest risk, and the reason night running is on the plan.
  • Last year's hours are estimated from your 2026 total. The watch's twelve-month total, when you have a moment, replaces the estimate.

The daily loop

Open your card, train, fill in the form at the bottom, press Copy, paste it into Claude and press Enter. That is all.

Everything else happens inside that paste: tomorrow's card is written, the rest of the week re-plans itself, the week is reviewed when it ends, and if the plan itself needs rebuilding you are asked once. You never type a command for any of it.

Tap what fits, skip what you like. On an ordinary day, Done as proposed plus the morning scales is a handful of taps. Every scale reads the same way — higher is better: legs 10 = fresh, sleep 5 = slept well.
Skipped it, or something hurts? That is a log too. Tap Skipped, or Anything hurt? → Yes and where. Reporting a problem costs you nothing: the plan is rebuilt around it, never withheld. Log the rest days too — a rest day logged as rest is data.

The movements, on video

Every strength card names each exercise with its weight, its sets, the one cue that matters and a video, written out in full so it works on a phone in the gym. The whole library, with the cues and the common mistakes: knowledge/exercise-library.md.
